# Shannon capacity of C7: a first computational probe

State of the art (checked 2026-11-03):
- Best published lower bound: α(C7^⊠5) ≥ 367, so Θ(C7) ≥ 367^{1/5} > 3.2578
  (Polak–Schrijver 2018, arXiv:1808.07438; computer search via the circular
  graph C_{108,382}).
- Preprint arXiv:2607.21517 (2026, unverified here): α(C7^⊠10) ≥ 134753, so
  Θ(C7) > 3.258020. It was found through iterative interaction with an LLM.
- Upper bound: Lovász ϑ(C7) < 3.3177. The exact value has been open since 1956.

Probe (`cyclic_search.py`): cyclic sets {t·(1, a, …, a^{m−1}) mod n} that map
losslessly to C7 through φ(x) = ⌊7x/n⌋, with required circular distance
k = ⌈2n/7⌉.

| m | largest n found | known α(C7^m) (lower bound) |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 10 | 10 (exact) |
| 3 | 31 | 33 |
| 4 | 94 | 108 |
| 5 | 299 (none in 300–400) | 367 |

Verdict: this lossless power-generator family is clearly weaker than the
known constructions, which use circular graphs with n/k slightly above 7/2 plus
a computer repair step. Beating the record needs those more general
constructions; an AI-assisted team is already working on them.

## Round 2: rethinking instead of giving up

Reformulation: a cyclic set {t·g mod n} is a lattice Z^m + Z·g/n in the torus. Lossless independence
means no nonzero lattice point in the cube (−2/7, 2/7)^m, so this is lattice packing of
cubes of side 2/7 in a torus. Θ(C7) < 3.5 because 3.5 is not an integer, so the cubes cannot tile.

| Approach | Size in C7^5 |
|---|---|
| Lossless, power generators (round 1) | 299 |
| Lossless, all cyclic lattices, annealing (`lattice_anneal.py`) | 315 (n = 7·45); n = 322, 329 stop at 1 violation |
| Subgroups of Z_7^5 = F7-linear codes avoiding {−1,0,1}^5 (`subgroup_anneal.py`) | 343 = 7³ |
| **Over-pack + exact repair** (`overpack_repair.py`, `repair_search.py`) | **365** |
| Published record (Polak–Schrijver 2018) | 367 |

Over-pack and repair is the record's mechanism. Take n = 382 points, more than
fit losslessly. Project each coordinate onto C7 by cutting the circle into 7 arcs,
then keep a maximum independent subset, solved exactly as a MILP and verified
pairwise. With uniform cuts this gives 355. Optimising the 7 cut points per
coordinate (unequal arcs) reached 365 in 6 minutes. Running now: a scan of all
(n, a) for n = 365…440 with g = (1, a, …, a⁴).

## Round 3: the upper-bound side (where new mathematics is needed)

Idea derived from our coincidence-inequality work: θ is a Cauchy–Schwarz bound
and also bounds the *entanglement-assisted* zero-error capacity (Beigi 2010). If
entanglement can reach θ(C7), no bound that also holds with entanglement can
beat θ. A better upper bound on Θ(C7) would then have to
be classical-only (rank-type, like Haemers). Concrete test: can entanglement
send 11 zero-error messages with two uses of C7? Classically α(C7²) = 10, and θ² = 11.007.

- `theta_cayley.py`: θ and Schrijver θ' of C7^k as Cayley-graph LPs (Fourier
  reduction plus symmetry orbits). For k = 1…4, θ' = θ = θ(C7)^k exactly
  (3.317667, 11.006916, 36.517283, 121.152193). So the θ' bound on the
  entanglement-assisted independence number (Briët et al.) does not rule out 11.
- `quantum_indep.py`: numerical search for a quantum independent set of size 11
  in C7² (11 projective measurements on C^d labelled by the 49 vertices, with
  orthogonality between confusable labels). Calibration: d = 1 finds the
  classical 10. Running for d = 2, 3, 4.

## Round 4: C7^4, where α is still open (108 ≤ α(C7^4) ≤ 115)

- The upper bound 115 appears to be the layer bound 2|I| ≤ 7·α(C7³) = 7·33,
  i.e. ⌊33·3.5⌋. This is our deduction; the original source is unverified.
- `alpha_c7_4_ilp.py`: exact ILP (HiGHS) with every block constraint implied by
  α(C7) = 3, α(C7²) = 10 and α(C7³) = 33, plus the 16-cliques and symmetry
  breaking. Running; the goal is a proven bound ≤ 114 or a set of size 109.
- `scan_m4.py`: over-pack and repair with uniform cuts reaches only 97 in C7^4.
- `linear_f7.py 13 8`: the best [13,8] F7 code found still contains 6 ± pairs of
  ternary codewords, so it gives no independent set of size 7^8.

## Round 5: exact search restricted to symmetry subgroups (`symmetry_search.py`)

Generic exact solvers fail here. HiGHS on α(C7^4) stayed at the 115.5 bound,
with a best set of 98, and could not even settle α(C7³) = 33 in 15 minutes
under CPU contention. The method that works is to:

1. pick a random subgroup H of Aut(C7^m), generated by 1–2 maps
   v ↦ P(s∘v) + t;
2. keep the H-orbits that have no internal conflict;
3. solve the maximum-weight independent set on the orbit conflict graph
   exactly (MILP);
4. verify the result pairwise;
5. repeat for thousands of subgroups.

Calibration: it finds α(C7³) = 33 in about 1 s (|H| = 6). Running: C7^4, aiming
for 109 (record 108, Vesel–Žerovnik 2002).

## Round 6: fusing θ with block constraints (`theta_blocks.py`): a null result that locates the barrier

For an independent set I and X = 1_I 1_Iᵀ/|I|, every block B with α(G[B]) ≤ c gives
the valid linear constraint Σ_{i,j∈B} X_ij ≤ c Σ_{i∈B} X_ii, because |B∩I|² ≤ c|B∩I|.
We added all product blocks {x,x+1}^s × C7^{k−s} to θ (exact LP over
translation-invariant X):

| k | θ + blocks | layer bound | true α |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 10.5 | 10.5 | 10 |
| 3 | 35.0 | 35.0 | 33 |

The fusion gives exactly the layer bound, never better. Reason: for a
translation-invariant X, the quadratic block constraint averages to a constraint
on Σ_d f(d) alone, and the θ part then only caps it. Any bound that beats the
layer bound (35 → 33 at k = 3, and hopefully 115 → ≤ 114 at k = 4) must
therefore **break translation symmetry**. One option is a Schrijver-type
three-point bound (fix one point of I; matrices invariant only under its
stabiliser of order 2^k·k!). That is the next upper-bound computation.

## Round 7: breaking translation symmetry (`theta_pointed.py`): still the layer bound

Pointed bound: α(G) = 1 + α(G − N[0]) for vertex-transitive G. On G − N[0] there
is no translation symmetry left. We computed θ with nonnegativity plus every
linear and quadratic block constraint (168 blocks, with capacities reduced by 1
for blocks containing the fixed point). For C7³ it gives 1 + 34.0000 = 35, again
exactly the layer bound and not the true 33.

**Conclusion of rounds 6–7.** θ-type relaxations with block information
reproduce the layer bound exactly but cannot see the loss 35 → 33. That loss is
combinatorial: maximum sets of consecutive layer pairs cannot be chained around
an odd cycle. The right tool is therefore a structural chaining analysis of the
maximum independent sets one level down (10-sets of C7² for k = 3, 33-sets of
C7³ for k = 4), not a stronger relaxation of this family.

## Round 8: layer chaining (`chain_k3.py`): the structural reason for 35 → 33

C7³ = C7² ⊠ C7, cut into 7 layers. I is independent iff each layer I_x is
independent in C7² and each union I_x ∪ I_{x+1} is independent (and disjoint).
The layer bound 35 = 7·10/2 would need every consecutive union to be a maximum
10-set split 5/5. That is a closed walk of length 7 in the **split graph**:
nodes are 5-sets, and two nodes are joined when their union is a maximum
independent set of C7².

- C7² has 1,796,859 independent sets, 980 of them maximum (size 10).
- Split graph: 60,760 five-sets and 123,480 edges, not bipartite.
- **Odd girth = 21, exact** (BFS on the bipartite double cover from all 60,760
  start nodes, `chain_k3_full.py`). A closed walk of length 7 would need an odd cycle of
  length ≤ 7, so 35 is impossible.
- Consequence (exact, given the girth): a perfect cyclic chain needs at least 21
  layers. For odd n, α(C7² ⊠ C_n) ≤ 5n − 1 when n < 21, and α(C7² ⊠ C_n) = 5n when
  n ≥ 21 (the 21-cycle, padded by back-and-forth steps, is a perfect chain).
  Prior art for this family has not been checked yet.

This is the analysis that θ-type relaxations cannot see (rounds 6–7). The plan for
C7^4 is the same construction one level up: 33-sets of C7³ and their
17/16 splits. If the 33-split structure also forbids the needed closed walk,
α(C7^4) ≤ 114, which would be a new bound.

**Prior art (checked after computing): this is a rediscovery.** Klavžar and Vesel,
*Discrete Applied Mathematics* 129 (2003) 449–460 ("rotagraphs", Theorem 6), use
exactly this method. They build the digraph of 5-splits of the 980 maximum
10-sets of C7² and find its shortest odd cycle. Their result,
α(C7 ⊠ C7 ⊠ C_{2k+1}) = 10k + 5 for k ≥ 10 and 10k + 4 for 4 ≤ k ≤ 9, agrees with
our independently computed odd girth 21. Our computation is therefore an
independent verification, not a new theorem.

What remains open is the method one level up: maximum 33-sets of C7³ and their
chains in C7^4 = C7³ ⊠ C7. As far as we found, it has not been carried out; it
would need the 33-sets (and near-maximum 32-sets) of C7³, which is much larger
than the 980 ten-sets of C7².

## Round 9: size-profile integer program for C7^4 (`profile_ip.py`): sizes alone allow 115

What 115 would require: six of the seven consecutive layer pairs are 33-sets of C7³
and one is a 32-set, which forces layer sizes exactly (16, 16, 17, 16, 17, 16, 17).

We view C7^4 = C7² ⊠ C7² as a 7 × 7 grid of C7² layers with sizes c_xy, subject to:
- every 2 × 2 block of layers sums to ≤ 10;
- every row, column, pair of adjacent rows or columns, diagonal and
  anti-diagonal sums to ≤ 33 (each of these is a copy of C7³, or of C7³ ⊠ K2).

The exact integer optimum is still 115 (LP 115.5). Size information alone cannot
lower the ceiling, so the obstruction, if there is one, lies in *which* sets can be
chained, as in Klavžar–Vesel. This requires the structure of the 33-sets of C7³.
Next: count and classify them with the deficit structure. 66 = Σ (consecutive pair
sizes) = 70 − 4, so at most 4 units of deficit, and at least 3 of the 7 pairs are
maximum 10-sets of C7².

## Round 10: arbitrary projections (`map_search.py`) and the 2026 context

**Context update (searched 2026-11-03).** Lower bounds on Θ(C7) moved in 2026 through
recursive constructions in high powers:
- Itty et al. (arXiv:2607.21517): 134753 in C7^10.
- Gao (arXiv:2607.27869): a recursive reformulation with product gadgets.
- arXiv:2608.30273: heterogeneous refinement, Θ(C7) ≥ 3.25883262 from the 500th power.

All of these start from the 367-set in C7^5. A larger base set in C7^5 would
therefore improve α(C7^5) and also feed every one of those recursions.

**New idea.** Replace the arc-cut projection Z_n → Z_7 with an arbitrary map
φ_i: Z_n → Z_7 per coordinate. This space contains every circular-graph
construction as a special case. Local search moves one point of one map; every
step gets an exact maximum-subset repair. We found no prior use of arbitrary
(non-monotone) projections. It starts from the 365 arc-cut solution: 60 s gave
no gain, and two 27-minute runs are in progress.

## Round 11: is α(C7^4) ≥ 115 possible? (`alpha115_feasibility.py`)

If |I| = 115, then along every coordinate the 7 layer sizes satisfy a_x + a_{x+1} ≤ 33 and
sum to 115 = 7·33/2 − 1/2. So exactly one consecutive pair has deficit 1, and the sizes are
forced to be (16, 16, 17, 16, 17, 16, 17) up to rotation and reflection. Rotations and
reflections are automorphisms acting independently on each coordinate, so without loss
of generality all four coordinates carry this exact pattern from x = 0, which gives 28
equality constraints.

Added on top: the 16-cliques and all block constraints ({x,x+1}^s blocks with
capacities 33, 10 and 3). The question becomes an exact feasibility ILP (HiGHS,
50-minute limit, running). If it is infeasible, then α(C7^4) ≤ 114, the first improvement
of the upper bound 115. A solver's infeasibility verdict is not a certificate:
before any claim it needs independent confirmation, by another solver, another
formulation, or a checkable proof.

The ILP (`alpha115_feasibility.py`) reached its 50-minute limit with no verdict
(0.37% of the tree explored). Switched to the tool that settled Keller's
conjecture: a SAT solver. `alpha115_sat.py` encodes the same question for
CaDiCaL 1.9.5 via PySAT: independence as edge clauses, the forced layer pattern
as exact cardinalities (sequential counters), and 2- and 3-block cardinality
constraints. 45-minute limit, running. An UNSAT answer would still need
independent confirmation (DRAT proof, or a second solver).

## Round 12: slicing C7^5 sets into C7^4 sets

Any independent set of C7^5, cut along a coordinate into 7 layers, gives independent
sets of C7^4 as unions of two consecutive layers. By averaging, a 367-set yields at
least ⌈2·367/7⌉ = 105, the value where our symmetry search stalled. But a single
pair can be much larger than the average. Slicing our verified 366-set gives
six different independent sets of size **107** in C7^4, each verified pairwise, along
coordinates 0, 1, 2 and 3. That beats our symmetric 105 and sits one below the
2002 record 108. The slices are now seeds for the exact large-neighbourhood search,
aiming for 108 or 109.

**Result.** From each of three 107-slices, the exact LNS reaches **108** within its first
iterations. That equals the best known lower bound for α(C7^4) (Vesel–Žerovnik 2002). All three
108-sets were verified pairwise; one is stored as `c7_4_108.npy`. The search keeps running for 109.
A pipeline that matches the 24-year-old record in seconds: C7^5 over-pack/repair, then slice to
C7^4, then exact LNS.

## Round 13: clique slicing as a general tool, and the 2026 sets

**Generalisation.** If I is independent in G ⊠ H and K is a clique of H, then the union of
I's layers over K is independent in G. Taking two adjacent layers is the case
H = C7, K = {x, x+1}.

- **2026 record set in C7^10 (Itty et al.):**
  I = B×B ∪ {(h_j(x), x)} ∪ {(x, v_j(x))}, with B = R minus 8 points and X a
  transformed copy of R. By analysis of the construction (no computation needed),
  no clique slice to C7^5 exceeds 367. Layers are B plus at most 8 points, or pieces of
  X, and the independence of R stops a clique from merging them.
- **Their C7^6 set of size 1120** (repository `nathanielitty/lower-bounds-for-shannon-capacity`,
  re-verified here). Two-layer slices to C7^5 reach only 325. 2 × 2-block slices to C7^4 reach
  exactly **108**. So two independent constructions (ours from C7^5, theirs from C7^6) both
  land on the 2002 value 108 for α(C7^4), which suggests 108 is very stable and possibly
  optimal. Their 108-slices are now additional seeds for the exact LNS search for 109.

## Round 14: layer-pattern rigidity of maximum sets (`structure108.py`): an empirical law

We took six 108-sets of C7^4: three from our C7^5 slices plus exact LNS, and three from 2×2-block
slices of the 2026 C7^6 set (Itty et al.). They are **pairwise inequivalent** under the full
automorphism group (order 921,984), and each has a **trivial stabiliser**. Yet in **every one of
the 4 coordinates of every set**, the 7 layer sizes form the same cyclic pattern:

    (15, 15, 16, 15, 16, 15, 16)    consecutive pair sums (30, 31, 31, 31, 31, 31, 31)

One level down, every maximum 33-set of C7³ found by the symmetry search (9 runs) has, in all 3
coordinates, the pattern

    (4, 4, 6, 4, 5, 4, 6)           consecutive pair sums (8, 10, 10, 9, 9, 10, 10)

**Observation.** 108 = ⌊7·31/2⌋. Maximum sets of C7^4 behave as if the *effective* capacity
of a pair of adjacent layers were 31, not α(C7³) = 33. This would explain why the layer bound
115 = ⌊7·33/2⌋ is so far off.

**Conjecture (empirical, unproved).** α(C7^4) = 108, and every maximum independent set of C7^4
has layer pattern (15, 15, 16, 15, 16, 15, 16) along every coordinate.

**Caveats.**
- The 33-sets come from one search method and may share a bias.
- The 108-sets come from two sources but only six samples.
- Proving the C7³ rigidity is a finite computation: for each admissible deficit pattern,
  a chain DP over the C7² independent sets of the given sizes. That would be the first step
  towards a proof strategy for C7^4.

## Round 15: where would a 109-set have to live? (`boundary109.py`)

If every pair of adjacent layers held ≤ 31 points, then 2|I| ≤ 7·31, so |I| ≤ 108. Hence any
109-set has an adjacent layer pair whose union J is an independent set of C7³ with |J| ≥ 32.

**Boundary-value test.** Take a maximum 33-set J of C7³ and split it between layers 0 and 1.
Solve exactly the maximum number of points the other 5 layers can hold (MILP, 1,715 variables).
With 5 minutes of local search over the split, the best is **67**, so the total is 100, far below
109. A full 33-slab is very costly: it forces the other layers far below their average. This is
consistent with the rigidity law (pairs ≤ 31 in maximum sets).

The exact LNS from all three 107-slices also ended at 108 after about 2,200 iterations each, with
no 109.

**Proof strategy (exact, finite).** α(C7^4) ≤ 108 holds iff, for every independent set J of C7³
with |J| ∈ {32, 33} (up to symmetry) and every split, the remaining 5 layers hold
≤ 108 − |J| + ... more precisely, the extension of J is ≤ 108. That is one MILP per J if the split
is left as a variable (layers 0 and 1 restricted to J). The open cost is enumerating the 32-sets
and 33-sets of C7³ up to symmetry.

## Round 16: the slab–capacity curve P_m(s) (`slab_curve.py`)

P_m(s) is the largest independent set of C7^m whose two adjacent layers 0 and 1 hold exactly
s points, so α(C7^m) = max_s P_m(s). The classical layer bound ignores the trade-off this
curve encodes. Values are exact MILP results, or best values found within 240 s, with the
dual bound in brackets.

| s | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 | 11 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P_3(s) | 32 | 32 | 32 [≤33] | 33 | 33 [≤34] | 33 [≤34] | 33 [≤35] | infeasible |
| rest = P − s | 28 | 27 | 26 | 26 | 25 | 24 | 23 | – |

For C7² the curve is P_2 = 9, 9, 10, 10 at s = 0…3, and 4 is infeasible.

In C7³ the curve is almost flat. Each extra slab point costs about one point elsewhere
(the rest falls by about 1 per unit of s), so a full slab of 10 still allows 33. In C7^4,
by contrast, a full 33-slab extended to only 100 in the boundary test (round 15), against
the 108 reached with slab 31. The cost per slab point appears to grow with the
dimension. Next: P_4(s) for s near the peak.

## Round 17: an efficiency "flow" and a tension with the rigidity law

Define the per-step efficiency η_m = 2·α(C7^m) / (7·α(C7^{m−1})), the fraction of the
layer bound that is actually achieved:

| m | α(C7^m) | η_m |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 10 | 0.952 |
| 3 | 33 | 0.943 |
| 4 | 108 (record) | 0.935 |
| 5 | ≥ 367 (record) | ≥ 0.971 |

η decreases smoothly up to m = 4 and then jumps at m = 5. Two readings:
- α(C7^4) > 108. A smooth η ≈ 0.95 would predict about 110, which contradicts the rigidity
  conjecture of round 14.
- C7^5 is arithmetically special: the cyclic n = 382 construction "resonates".

**Test.** The 367 record (R, extracted from the 2026 repository: the union of the layers over
{r₀, q₀} of their C7^10 set, verified independent) has two-layer slices of at most **107**,
like our 366-set. Exact LNS from three such slices is running.

## Round 18: the defect law and a law-guided search (`lns_balanced.py`)

**Defect law (empirical).** α(C7^m) = ⌊7/2 · (α(C7^{m−1}) − δ_m)⌋, where δ_m is the per-pair loss
caused by not closing the odd 7-cycle of layers. From the known values:

| m | δ_m |
|---|---|
| 2 | 0 |
| 3 | ≈ 0.57 |
| 4 | ≈ 2 |
| 5 | ≤ 3 |

δ_5 = 3 reproduces 367 exactly; a smoother δ_5 ≈ 2.1 would allow 370–371.

**Supporting signal.**
- The maximum sets we know (C7³, C7^4) are layer-rigid and balanced.
- The 367 record (`c7_5_367_record.npy`, extracted from the 2026 repository and verified) is
  *not*: per-coordinate layers range from 50 to 55, and adjacent-pair sums from 101 to 107.
- Our heuristic reading: non-rigidity signals slack.

**Law-guided search.** `lns_balanced.py` runs exact LNS that also accepts equal-size moves
reducing layer imbalance. It navigates the plateau towards balanced configurations instead
of stopping at the local optimum. Running from the 367 record and from our 366.

P_4(s) by generic MILP is not informative: s = 29 gave 99 found and bound 140 after 600 s. The
computation was stopped. The slab curve in C7^4 needs structured methods, e.g. LNS with the
slab size fixed, or the boundary-value formulation of round 15.

**Universality check on C5** (exact values α(C5^m) = 2, 5, 10, 25 for m = 1…4). The same defect
formula α_m = ⌊5/2·(α_{m−1} − δ_m)⌋ gives δ_2 = 0, δ_3 ∈ (0.6, 1], δ_4 = 0. The defect is not
smooth: it vanishes in even powers, where the perfect lattice code {(x, 2x)} of C5² exists. We
read the C7 efficiency jump at m = 5 the same way, as a "resonant" dimension whose near-perfect
structure is the cyclic n = 382 lattice. A hypothesis to test: map lattice-packing densities
across m to locate the resonant dimensions of C7.
